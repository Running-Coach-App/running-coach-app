# SPRINT-08 retrospective

Held 2026-11-19, on the sprint that ran from 2026-11-16 to 2026-11-21.

## What Went Well

- **A phone that nobody on the team had chosen for testing found five defects in one afternoon**, four of which no unit test could reach. The worst was [#253](https://github.com/Running-Coach-App/running-coach-app/issues/253): turning off `Usage data` made the app die on every launch, so the one privacy control the `Customer` asked for was the control that bricked the product. It is fixed, verified on hardware through three cold launches, and shipped. This is `SPRINT-07`'s own action — "before claiming a behaviour works, test it on a device nobody on the team chose" — doing exactly what it was written for, in its first sprint.
- **The team found its own proof wrong and said so in public.** [`QR-08`](../../docs/quality-requirements/qr/QR-08.md)'s scanner reported `PASSED` while the artifact on the releases page held `INTERNET`, was debuggable, and permitted cleartext — and the README claimed the opposite. [#248](https://github.com/Running-Coach-App/running-coach-app/issues/248) records the gap and [#249](https://github.com/Running-Coach-App/running-coach-app/pull/249) corrected the claims, rather than the scanner being quietly widened so the number stayed green.
- **A bad toolchain change was reverted in hours, exactly.** The Gradle 9.9.1 bump caused two intermittent build failures; [#261](https://github.com/Running-Coach-App/running-coach-app/pull/261) put the wrapper and the lockfile back together, with `git diff` against the pre-bump commit empty, and recorded the hold where the bump is configured so next week does not replay it.
- **The device checks were written so they could fail honestly.** Three scripted attempts produced output that looked like a result and was not — a tap on a moved coordinate, a regex anchored on the wrong character, a redirect reading a device path locally. The versions that stand abort when their preconditions are not met: the microphone setting is read back from the app's own preferences, and the session is checked alive at every sample, so a session that dies early cannot be reported as a passing check.

## What Did Not

- **Three decisions in one day were made on checks that were green when they ran and meaningless when they landed.** [#247](https://github.com/Running-Coach-App/running-coach-app/pull/247) was merged on a check from 21:19 the previous evening against a different tree, and the bump it carried then failed twice. [#257](https://github.com/Running-Coach-App/running-coach-app/pull/257) was passing on a base two toolchain bumps old. [#259](https://github.com/Running-Coach-App/running-coach-app/pull/259) failed for the same reason and went green the moment its branch was updated. **Root cause:** nothing requires a branch to be up to date before it merges, so whether a green tick is still true is a judgement a person makes from a timestamp — and nobody makes it reliably. It is the same shape as the broken documentation link that reached `main` in Week 7 past a failing `Docs` check.
- **A requirement was rewritten without reading the one that already specified it.** [`FR-002`](../../docs/functional-requirements/fr/FR-002.md) was edited to make the stats cadence the runner's, which [`FR-012`](../../docs/functional-requirements/fr/FR-012.md) had specified since Sprint 5 — and before that the two contradicted each other outright, one forbidding the setting the other required. **Root cause:** `US-11`'s `Specified by` section said "FR-012, to be written", which was false, and nothing checks a story's claim about a requirement against the requirement's own file. `FR-012` was cited from nowhere but the index, so the contradiction sat unseen for three sprints.
- **An issue was opened on an inference about the room, not about the code.** [#250](https://github.com/Running-Coach-App/running-coach-app/issues/250) asserted that six recorded questions came from the coach hearing itself, because the phone "lay on a desk with nobody speaking to it" — which the author could not observe down a USB cable. A person had been asking them. **Root cause:** the event log was read as a complete record of what happened in the room, when it records only what the product did. The issue was withdrawn, and later reopened on a direct measurement that did show the fault, which is the right ending and three hours later than it needed to be.
