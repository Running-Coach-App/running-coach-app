# SPRINT-07 retrospective

The fourth retrospective of the project. It covers `SPRINT-07`, 9 to 15 November 2026, whose goal was that a runner who has never seen the product can be handed it and asked to use it, and that the team can tell from a written test, rather than from being in the room, where the product loses them.

The sprint got half of that goal in a way nobody planned. The written test exists and no session has run; but the `Customer` handed himself the product, and what the team learned from twelve minutes of his running is worth more than everything else the sprint produced.

## What Went Well

| What went well |
| --- |
| **Somebody outside the team used the product.** After four weeks of the same action point being carried, the `Customer` built from `main`, installed on his own phone and ran with it. Seven weeks of claims about this product had been made by the people who wrote it; now there is one real data point, and it is worth more than the claims. |
| **The project completed its first story.** [`US-03`](https://github.com/Running-Coach-App/running-coach-app/issues/39) is accepted — he heard the effort cues without looking and was happy with them — which makes `SPRINT-07` the first sprint with a non-zero actual velocity after `SPRINT-04` and `SPRINT-06` both recorded zero. Three points of twenty-seven is not much, and it is the first evidence that anything this team built works for somebody who did not build it. |
| The action the sprint inherited twice is done, and done as research rather than as recollection. Google's own Core app quality and Android vitals pages were read and are cited; the useful finding — that the checklist gates nothing and the vitals thresholds are the part with consequences — is the opposite of what the team would have assumed, and the `Customer`'s own prediction about per-phone-model hiding turned out to be the 8% threshold almost word for word. |
| **The `Customer` verified a quality requirement himself.** He read the repository's security work and found nothing wrong with it: "in terms of security, and other things, it was all fine... I didn't find anything." [`QR-08`](../../docs/quality-requirements/qr/QR-08.md) is the first requirement in this project checked by somebody who is not on the team. |
| Three of Google's criteria became requirements with tests instead of paragraphs in a document: [`QR-06`](../../docs/quality-requirements/qr/QR-06.md), [`QR-07`](../../docs/quality-requirements/qr/QR-07.md), and [`QR-08`](../../docs/quality-requirements/qr/QR-08.md) with the scanner the `Customer` asked for. |
| The scanner was tested by trying to break it, not by reading it. Three separate bugs that made it print `PASSED` when it should have failed were found that way, and the workflow now performs the sabotage itself on every run, so the test cannot rot into a check that always passes. |
| **An action carried four times was finally done, and done properly.** Every one of this meeting's six decisions, [`DEC-034`](../../docs/decisions.md#dec-034) to [`DEC-039`](../../docs/decisions.md#dec-039), has a task issue opened in the same change that recorded it. `SPRINT-04`, `SPRINT-05` and `SPRINT-06` all asked for this and none of them did it. |
| `mkdocs build --strict` now runs on pull requests, closing an action carried since `SPRINT-05`. It earned its place in the same week: two links leaving `docs_dir` were written and caught by hand, which is the fourth time that mistake has been made and the first time a check will catch the next one. |
| [`QR-09`](../../docs/quality-requirements/qr/QR-09.md)'s bar was set before any data exists, with the reasoning beside it and an explicit statement that it is not changed afterwards. The temptation it avoids is real: the team is measuring its own product and will be the one writing down whether it passed. |

## What Did Not

| Problem | Root cause |
| --- | --- |
| **The product failed its first real user, on everything that makes it the product.** The pocket session did not work at all: no spoken command answered, neither volume key did anything, and the `Customer` had to take the phone out. Pace was never spoken. Twelve minutes cost 6% of his battery. His verdict was that as it stands it is not useful for running. | Every claim about pocket use had been tested only by the three people who wrote it, on the two phones they happened to own, and [`QR-03`](../../docs/quality-requirements/qr/QR-03.md) — the requirement that holds exactly this behaviour — has never had an implemented test. The team had no mechanism that could have caught any of it, and the first device it did not choose broke the central promise. The battery cost was the same blindness in another form: the product was never measured against another app on the same phone. |
| **All seven pull requests merged this sprint were authored by one member.** The other two reviewed, merged, and carried the customer meeting, which the definition of done requires and which is real work, but nothing that landed on `main` was written by them. | The sprint's work was picked up by whoever was already holding the context rather than assigned at planning, and the deadline made that feel efficient each time it happened. It is not sustainable and it is not what the course is for: two members are not practising the thing the project exists to teach them. |
| **For the third sprint running, the plan recorded points the team could not finish:** 27 planned, 3 delivered, and 19 of the planned points sat on stories blocked on an install. `SPRINT-06`'s retrospective had already written the action forbidding exactly this. | The six carried stories were moved into the `SPRINT-07` milestone at `SPRINT-06`'s close, before the action existed, and nobody took the blocked four back out. An action that requires somebody to undo a thing already done is weaker than one that changes what gets done next time. The block did clear this sprint — he installed, and [`US-03`](https://github.com/Running-Coach-App/running-coach-app/issues/39) closed because of it — and the other four still did not, because the product failed on his phone. A forecast that is only right by accident is still not a forecast. |

Three further problems are recorded here without an action, because the course allows four and three of this sprint's four are consumed by actions carried for the third and fourth time.

The scanner reached CI with two code paths that meant different things, and the pull request body asserted they meant the same thing; only one path had ever been run, because the real tool was not installed locally. The `UT-01` instrumentation is designed and not implemented, which puts the one thing gating every counted session into the same sprint as the sessions. And the authorship imbalance above has no action against it, which is the most uncomfortable of the three; it goes to `SPRINT-08`'s retrospective with this sprint named as where it came from.

That three of four action slots are filled by re-listed actions is, again, the finding this retrospective would make about itself.
