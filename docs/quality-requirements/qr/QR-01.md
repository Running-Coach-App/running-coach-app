# QR-01: Speak The Stats Line On Time

A stats line that falls due during a session starts playing within two seconds of the moment it is due.

- **Status:** Active
- **Characteristic:** Performance Efficiency
- **Sub-characteristic:** Time Behaviour
- **Business importance:** High
- **Technical risk:** Medium

## Scenario

### Source

The session clock inside the running app reaches the moment a stats line is due.

### Stimulus

The coach has to speak the elapsed time, the pace, and the distance while the runner is moving.

### Environment

A session is running with the screen off and the phone in a pocket, on a phone on API 26 or newer, with the location fix arriving from the phone's own receiver and the platform voice already initialised.

### Artifact

The spoken output of the running session.

### Response

The coach shall begin speaking the stats line without waiting for the next location update.

### Response Measure

In at least 9 of 10 measured session ticks, the first word of the stats line is audible within two seconds of the moment the line fell due.

## QRT-01

A stats line due during a running session begins playing within two seconds of the moment it falls due.

- **Status:** Active
- **Level:** `scripted`
- **Runs at:** `gradle :app:testDebugUnitTest --tests '*SessionEngineTest.theFirstStatsLineComesOnTheThreeMinuteCadenceAndMissingStatsAreNotInvented*'` on a development machine, timed against the engine's own clock.

### Procedure

1. From a clean checkout, run `gradle :app:testDebugUnitTest` on a development machine with JDK 17.
2. Note the wall-clock duration the test report records for the cadence test, which drives the engine past the three-minute mark and collects every line it returns.
3. Read from the report how many lines the engine produced and at which elapsed times it produced them.

**Pass when:** the engine returns the stats line for the tick at which it falls due, with no tick in between returning nothing, and the test's own duration stays under two seconds, so the decision to speak costs less than the bound the requirement sets.
