# Consent to take part in a usability test

The public template. Every counted `UT-01` session has one of these recorded before it starts, with the placeholders filled in and the participant's agreement given in a durable form: a signed sheet, an email, or a message the team can show the course instructors. The filled-in records are kept in the private usability-data repository and never in this one.

The template is the same text the participants are given. The placeholders here are left unfilled on purpose.

---

## What this is

The Running Coach App is a student project built by team 4 for the ITPD course at Innopolis University. It is an Android app that coaches a runner out loud while they run, so that they do not have to look at their phone.

We are asking you to try it on a short run so we can find out where it is confusing. **We are testing the app, not you.** There is no right answer and nothing you can get wrong: if you cannot work something out, that is a result we need, and it is the app's fault rather than yours.

The session takes about 40 minutes. It is outdoors, on or beside the university campus, in daylight. You will be asked to run or jog for about five minutes with our phone in your pocket and headphones on, and then to answer some questions. You can stop at any point, for any reason, without giving one.

You do not need to install anything, and you do not need to use your own phone.

## What we record

- An **audio recording** of the session, which is what you say and what the app says. It is made on a separate device and it stays private.
- The **observer's notes**: what you did, what you tried, where you stopped, how long each task took, and anything you say out loud while you work.
- The **app's own record of the run**: when the session started, when the coach spoke, whether the phone had a GPS fix, and when the session stopped. It carries no words you said and nothing about you — the app records that a question was asked, never what it was.
- Your answers to the questions we ask before and after the tasks.

Everything above is filed under a code — `P-01`, `P-02`, and so on — rather than under your name. The list that says which code is yours is kept privately, separately from everything else, and is destroyed with the rest of the private material.

## What becomes public

A sanitized summary goes into the project's public repository on GitHub, where anyone can read it: what the tasks were, whether they were completed, how long they took, the scores you gave, and the themes across all the sessions. It is written about `P-nn`, not about you.

**It never contains** your name, your email address, your phone number, your address, your photograph, your voice, the recording, or anything else that identifies you. A quote from what you said is published only if you agree to it separately below.

## Who else sees the private material

The three members of team 4, and the course instructors who mark the project. Nobody else.

## What we use

A phone running the app, a separate audio recorder, a stopwatch, and a written notes sheet. Software tools, including an AI coding assistant, are used by the team to write the project's documents; anything from this session that is given to such a tool is the sanitized summary above and never the recording, the private notes, or the list of names. Any copy a tool holds is deleted when the course is graded.

## How long we keep it

Until the course is graded, and no longer. After grading, the team deletes the recording, the private notes, the answer sheets, and the list that joins your code to your name, together with the whole private repository and its history. The sanitized public summary stays, because it is part of the project's coursework.

## Your choices

You can stop the session at any point. You can withdraw afterwards — tell any team member, and we delete your recording, your notes, and your answers, and we retire your code so nothing is filed under it. You do not have to give a reason, and nothing happens to you either way. Withdrawal is possible up until the course is graded, after which the material no longer exists to delete.
